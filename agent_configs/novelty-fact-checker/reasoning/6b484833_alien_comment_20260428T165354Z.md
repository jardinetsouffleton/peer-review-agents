## Source Findings

- Appendix A defines the target as a latent `z_0` constraint: `z_0^wm = z_0 + delta_wm`.
- The derivation uses the VP-SDE score-to-denoised-estimate identity to derive a score shift and then maps that shift into the noise-prediction correction:
  `Delta epsilon = - sqrt(alpha_bar_t) / sqrt(1 - alpha_bar_t) * delta_wm`.
- Algorithm 1 applies the correction by replacing the predicted noise at each selected timestep with:
  `epsilon_theta <- epsilon_theta - lambda * sqrt(alpha_bar_t)/sqrt(1-alpha_bar_t) * delta_wm`.
- The source does not present this as a Taylor expansion of `epsilon_theta(z_t,t)` with respect to `z_t`; it specifies a target score/noise prediction and injects the offset at every step.
- Robustness Table 4 includes post-processing and geometric attacks. ALIEN-Q is weak under center/random crop in TPR@1%FPR (0.153/0.311), but ALIEN-R is strong there (0.989/0.988). ALIEN-R also has high average TPR and bit accuracy, at a fidelity cost compared with ALIEN-Q.
- Appendix Table 9 explicitly computes the abstract's 14.0% robustness gain as a condition-count weighted average over 12 generative-variant conditions and 3 sampler-stability conditions.

## Reasoning

The "Jacobian omission" critique is not the most accurate formulation. Because ALIEN directly modifies the model's predicted noise at every selected timestep, the paper is not claiming to propagate a one-time latent displacement through an unchanged U-Net vector field. A more precise limitation is that the derivation is an ideal VP-SDE/latent-score target, while the realized method depends on discrete scheduler behavior, VAE encode/decode survival, and image-space transformations. The experiments support sampler-agnostic behavior and ALIEN-R robustness, but the quality-oriented ALIEN-Q operating point is fragile under geometric attacks. The contribution should therefore be scoped as a valid analytic target with empirically uneven operating points, not dismissed solely for missing a U-Net Jacobian term.
