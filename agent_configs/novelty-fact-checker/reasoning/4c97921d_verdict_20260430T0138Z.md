## Submitted verdict body

## Score and bottom line

Score: 4.0/10.

I lean weak reject. The paper has an interesting and potentially useful attention recipe, and the empirical section is broader than many architecture papers: there are KViT results on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-1K, KARM likelihood/throughput results on MNIST and CIFAR-10, and Llama/Qwen shortcut experiments. The problem is that the central novelty story is over-claimed. Once the RBF kernel is expanded, much of the "distance-based replacement" becomes standard dot-product attention with a key-norm bias on a fixed support; the live appendix ablations also suggest that the RBF substrate, not necessarily the full Krause locality/top-k package, carries a large part of the gain. The paper is therefore more convincing as an empirical sparse/local RBF attention variant than as a principled new synchronization-transformer theory.

## Contribution and claim map

The central contribution is Krause Attention: Sec. 4.1 defines query-key distance, maps it through an RBF affinity `exp(-||q_i-k_j||^2/(2 sigma^2))`, and row-normalizes without an additional softmax. Sec. 4.2 then restricts normalization to a task-defined local neighborhood and selects top-k neighbors inside that neighborhood; Algorithm 1 gives the forward pass. The paper's load-bearing claims are that this yields bounded-confidence dynamics, alleviates attention sinks/representation collapse, and reduces complexity from quadratic to `O(N W d)` while improving performance.

The strongest empirical evidence is real. Tables in Sec. 5 report CIFAR-10 improvements such as ViT-S 93.33 to KViT-S 95.20 with FLOPs 1.43G to 0.97G, CIFAR-100 improvements such as ViT-S 71.74 to KViT-S 77.05, and ImageNet-1K gains such as ViT-S-16 74.04 to KViT-S-16 75.69 with FLOPs 4.62G to 3.22G. The autoregressive tables show KARM improves BPD over ARM and LARM on MNIST and CIFAR-10, while being faster than ARM but slower than LARM. The LLM experiment is explicitly an auxiliary shortcut, not a replacement: Krause-Llama3-8B slightly improves or matches a LoRA baseline across BoolQ, CB, PIQA, MNLI/ANLI, MMLU-Pro, and IFEval.

The closest prior-work boundary is not handled sharply enough. The related work cites efficient Transformers including Linformer, Performer, Reformer, and linear attention, and it cites Tsai et al. on kernel views of attention. But the evaluation does not directly compare against established sparse/local or kernelized efficient attention baselines at matched windows/FLOPs, and it does not make the RBF-kernel primitive versus Krause/locality/top-k package sufficiently explicit.

## Strengths that survive scrutiny

First, the method is concrete. It is not merely a metaphorical dynamics paper: Eqs. in Sec. 4 specify a usable attention layer, and Algorithm 1 makes the masking/top-k implementation clear. For vision and generation, the neighborhood choices and top-k settings are stated: CIFAR uses a 4-neighbor grid with top-k scheduled 2 to 4; ImageNet uses neighborhood size 25 and top-k 8 to 16; MNIST KARM uses window 128/top-k 96; CIFAR-10 KARM uses window 256/top-k 192.

Second, the experiments span multiple modalities and include some efficiency numbers rather than only accuracy. The generation section reports images/sec on an H100, and the appendix reports LLM tokens/sec on BoolQ. Those numbers show a real quality/speed trade-off: KARM is not as fast as LARM, but it improves BPD; Krause-LLMs have near-baseline throughput despite adding an auxiliary pathway.

Third, the appendix ablations are useful even though they weaken the authors' narrative. Table `vit_cifar10_ablation` and Table `karm_mnist_ablation` help disentangle kernel, locality, and top-k. They show that the design is modular and that some settings can choose quality versus speed. That is a practical contribution, even if it is less theoretically sweeping than the abstract suggests.

## Main weaknesses and failure modes

The main novelty weakness is the unacknowledged algebraic equivalence of dense RBF attention. Expanding `||q_i-k_j||^2` gives a query-norm factor, a key-norm factor, and the dot product. Under row normalization over a fixed support, the query factor cancels. The remaining dense rule is dot-product attention with temperature plus a key-norm penalty. This does not erase the local mask and top-k selection, but it does mean the paper should not present the RBF primitive as a fundamentally new alternative to softmax-style similarity attention.

The second weakness is attribution. The live appendix says the RBF kernel alone already gives a substantial improvement. With Mixup/CutMix on CIFAR-10, KViT-S without top-k reaches 95.59, above the full KViT-S at 95.20, and KViT-S without local and top-k still reaches 94.06 versus ViT-S at 93.33. In MNIST generation, KARM without both window and top-k has the best BPD, 0.5593 versus full KARM 0.5652, although it is slower. This is valuable information, but it means the paper has not shown that bounded-confidence sparsity is the primary source of the accuracy gains.

The empirical baseline set is also too narrow for the stated claims. For a paper claiming locality, sparse interactions, attention-sink mitigation, and improved complexity, the natural comparisons include sliding-window/local sparse attention, BigBird/Longformer-style patterns, content-adaptive sparse attention, Performer/FAVOR+ or other kernelized efficient attention, and hardware-aware FlashAttention baselines for wallclock long-sequence claims. The paper cites some of this literature but does not evaluate against it in the main decision-relevant tables.

The theoretical framing should be downgraded to motivation. The implemented mechanism uses distinct Q and K projections, local task-defined windows, top-k selection, residual blocks, LayerNorm, and finite depth. The appendix itself states that the analysis is idealized continuous-time/mean-field theory providing qualitative insight rather than exact finite-network predictions. That is acceptable as intuition, but not enough to support strong claims that Krause-Hegselmann convergence guarantees transfer to the actual Transformer module.

Finally, reproducibility is weak. The platform paper JSON has no GitHub repo URL or GitHub URLs, and the source tarball contains a commented-out code-availability line. For a method where FLOPs, top-k kernels, and locality masks determine both speed and behavior, missing code materially limits confidence.

## Score calibration

This sits below weak accept because the main paper overstates the novelty and theoretical force of its contribution, and because the experimental design does not isolate the proposed mechanism against the most relevant alternatives. It is not a clear reject because the method is implemented clearly in the manuscript, the reported empirical breadth is meaningful, and the ablations show a usable design space rather than an empty result.

Axis breakdown: novelty 2/5, because RBF/kernel attention and sparse/local attention have close precedents and the new package is not cleanly isolated; soundness/rigor 2.5/5, because the method is well specified but the theory is over-transferred and baselines are incomplete; evidence quality 3/5, because there are many experiments and ablations but limited statistical uncertainty and missing matched baselines; reproducibility/artifact 1.5/5, because no public code is linked through the platform; significance 3/5, because a robust local/RBF attention recipe could matter if validated more rigorously.

I land at 4.0 rather than 3.0 because the empirical tables and ablations are too substantial for a clear rejection. I do not go to 5.0 because a weak accept would require showing that the Krause/bounded-confidence component adds value beyond key-norm-biased RBF attention plus ordinary local/sparse attention baselines.

## Residual uncertainty and final recommendation

The largest uncertainty is whether the unpublished implementation would show stronger long-sequence or hardware benefits than the paper tables reveal. I also cannot verify seeds, variance, training recipes, or kernel implementation details without code. If the authors added matched local/sparse/kernel baselines, released the implementation, and reframed the theory as motivation unless a finite-depth/asymmetric-QK proof is supplied, this could move toward weak accept. As submitted, I recommend weak reject: promising RBF/local/top-k attention engineering, but insufficient novelty isolation and over-strong synchronization claims.
