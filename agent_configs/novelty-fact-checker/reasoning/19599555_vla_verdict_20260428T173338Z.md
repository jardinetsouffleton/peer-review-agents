## Evidence for acceptance

The paper offers a useful diagnostic benchmark design for VLA policies:

- It defines a multi-object picking task where the manipulation primitive is held fixed while the instructed object changes.
- It introduces a spatial task ladder: small jitter, medium jitter, large jitter, and full random placement.
- It reports decomposed metrics: Success, Grasp-anything, and Reach, where Reach is within 5 cm of the instructed object.
- It evaluates modern VLA policies, including SmolVLA and pi_0.5.

The strongest empirical evidence is not the intermediate jitter regimes but the hard cases:

- Full random SmolVLA: Success 2, Grasp-any 12, Reach 4. This supports a collapse of instruction-conditioned target selection when region cues are removed.
- Held-out object-region pairings under small jitter: Success drops from 44 to 0, Grasp-any from 75 to 31, and Reach from 100 to 2. This is the cleanest grounding result because object and region marginals are observed but object-location combinations are held out.
- Object-count ablation: in full random, one-object success rises substantially relative to two/five-object settings, supporting the interpretation that multi-object target selection, not only primitive execution, is load-bearing.

These results support the paper's methodological contribution: decomposed metrics and task ladders can reveal failures hidden by aggregate benchmark success.

## Evidence against stronger acceptance

Several issues cap confidence:

1. Cross-table inconsistency:
   - Table 1 reports SmolVLA Medium Jitter 42 and Large Jitter 48, average 45.5.
   - Table 2 reports SmolVLA Medium Jitter 37 and Large Jitter 41.
   - Table 4 shows the 37->42 and 41->48 data-scaling endpoints, implying Table 1 silently mixes post-scaling values while Table 2 reports baseline values.

2. Intermediate jitter metric ambiguity:
   - Reach is defined as ending within 5 cm of the instructed object.
   - SmolVLA Large Jitter has Reach 100 but Success 41; Medium Jitter has Reach 93 but Success 37.
   - These regimes look partly like reach/grasp completion failures or region-conditioned localization, not purely wrong-object grounding.

3. Incomplete pi_0.5 ladder:
   - pi_0.5 is only evaluated at Large Jitter and Full Random due to computational constraints.
   - This limits cross-model conclusions about the whole task ladder.

4. Statistical and reproducibility limits:
   - Results are reported over 100 evaluation episodes after hyperparameter grid search, with no multi-seed variance or confidence intervals.
   - Metadata provides no GitHub code artifact.

5. Scope and novelty:
   - The method is best framed as an actionable diagnostic framework rather than discovery of a new phenomenon, since spatial shortcutting and decomposed robot evaluation have prior context.
   - The task scope is multi-object picking, so broader VLA conclusions should be bounded.

## Score calibration

I choose `5.2`, a weak accept but close to the boundary. The task-ladder and decomposed-metric framing is useful and the cleanest hard-regime evidence is compelling enough to clear a low weak-accept threshold. However, the reporting inconsistency, lack of variance, no artifact, incomplete second-model ladder, and overbroad intermediate-regime grounding narrative prevent a stronger score.
