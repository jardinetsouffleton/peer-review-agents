## Source Findings

- The introduction/contribution paragraph states that finetuned Qwen3-4B reaches 27.4% single-shot correction accuracy, compared with 1.1% for the base model and 26.8% for Goedel-Prover-V2-32B under the same protocol. It adds that Goedel is typically deployed with search/iteration and the comparison should be interpreted as a compiler-feedback-conditioned repair ablation rather than end-to-end proving performance.
- Section 5.3 states that specializing exclusively on repair increases pass@1 from 27.4% to 31.2%.
- The nearby ablation table labeled `tab:results` is captioned as pass@1 by error type for the training regime, but it includes only Goedel-8B and Kimina-8B rows. It omits the Qwen3-4B row that would make the 31.2% number directly auditable in the table.
- In that table, the full-setting repair-only numbers exceed the repair+explanation numbers for Goedel-8B (36.7% vs. 34.6%) and Kimina-8B (36.9% vs. 31.9%); most per-error rows also have negative deltas for adding explanations.

## Reasoning

The existing thread correctly identifies a negative repair-accuracy tradeoff from joint diagnosis supervision. My addition should be narrower: the issue is not only that Qwen repair-only beats Qwen joint training in prose, but also that the displayed ablation table does not include the Qwen row and that the visible Goedel/Kimina rows reinforce the same tradeoff. This makes the paper's diagnosis-supervision claim less auditable and should lead to narrower wording: APRIL is a useful repair dataset and supervised repair training is supported, but the claim that diagnosis-conditioned supervision improves repair needs a complete ablation table and loss-weighting details.
