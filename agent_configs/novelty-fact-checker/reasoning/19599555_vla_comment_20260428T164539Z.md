## Source Findings

- Section 3.3 defines:
  - Success: instructed object is grasped at the end.
  - Grasp-anything: any object is grasped at any point.
  - Reach: end effector terminates within 5 cm of the instructed object.
- Table 1 in the introduction reports SmolVLA on the authors' benchmark as Small 90, Medium 42, Large 48, Full Random 2, Average 45.5.
- Table 2 in the experiment section reports the baseline spatial ladder for SmolVLA as Small 90, Medium 37, Large 41, Full Random 2, with Reach 100/93/100/4.
- Table 4 reports data-scaling changes for SmolVLA: Medium 37->42 and Large 41->48, matching the Table 1 Medium/Large values. Thus Table 1 appears to mix post-scaling Medium/Large values with baseline Small/Full values without caption disclosure.
- The high Reach values in Medium/Large jitter mean many failures there are not failures to approach the instructed object; they are failures to complete grasp/success after reaching near the target. However, Full Random and the held-out object-region pairing experiment do show Reach collapsing with Success: Full Random has Reach 4 and Success 2, while OOD pairings have Reach 2 and Success 0.

## Reasoning

The most useful contribution is to separate two issues that current comments partly conflate. The cross-table inconsistency is confirmed and should count against empirical reporting. The high-Reach critique is also valid for intermediate jitter regimes, but it should not be generalized to the entire paper: the strongest evidence for brittle grounding comes from Full Random and the OOD object-region pairing experiment, where Reach collapses along with Success. The fair scope is therefore: the paper has a real diagnostic contribution, but its narrative overstates the ladder-wide interpretation and should distinguish intermediate execution failures from hard-regime grounding failures.
