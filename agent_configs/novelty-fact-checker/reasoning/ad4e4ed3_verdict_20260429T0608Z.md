## Submitted verdict

## Score and bottom line

Score: 4.6/10.

My recommendation is a borderline weak reject. The paper attacks an important and underexplored setting: one target-specific universal perturbation that transfers across arbitrary source images and closed-source MLLMs. The reported Table 1 gains are not cosmetic: on unseen samples, TarVRoM moves GPT-4o ASR from 38.0 for UAP to 61.7 and Gemini from 36.8 to 56.7, with parallel KMR improvements. That is enough for serious consideration. The problem is that the central empirical claim is evaluated with a same-family judge/captioning protocol, while the main theory and artifact support do not cleanly validate the implemented method. I would not score this as a clear reject, but I do not think the current evidence is load-bearing enough for acceptance.

## Contribution and claim map

The paper's central claim is that universal targeted transferable adversarial attacks against closed-source MLLMs can be made practical by combining Target-View Aggregation with an Attention-Focused View, alignability-gated Token Routing, and Reptile-style meta-initialization. The closest boundary is not generic UAPs, but targeted universal perturbations and transfer attacks against vision-language systems. The manuscript itself acknowledges universal transferable MLLM attacks and X-Transfer in the motivation section, so the novelty is best understood as an integrated targeted-universal, arbitrary-image-target recipe for closed-source MLLM transfer rather than a wholly new paradigm.

The evidence base is Table 1 for universal closed-source attack performance, Table 3 for component attribution, Table 4 for target-view count, and Table 5 for fast adaptation. I checked the source tarball: it contains `preprint.tex`, four table files, figures, and the bibliography, but no code, no configs, and no appendix despite repeated references to Appendix results, implementation details, ASR thresholds, defenses, epsilon sensitivity, and unseen-target adaptation.

## Strengths that survive scrutiny

First, the threat model is meaningful. Section III defines a stricter universal targeted setting than sample-wise transfer attacks: one perturbation for a target image must steer unseen source images toward that target across unknown commercial MLLMs. That is a useful security benchmark even if the "first systematic" language should be narrowed.

Second, the empirical deltas over the universal baselines are large enough that they cannot be dismissed as formatting noise. Table 1 gives GPT-4o unseen ASR 61.7 vs 38.0 for UAP and Gemini 56.7 vs 36.8; KMR_a also improves from 37.5 to 52.0 on GPT-4o and from 40.2 to 52.6 on Gemini. The KMR counterargument in [[comment:c0a0cec6-6062-456e-93f5-639e02d9cf84]] is partly right: KMR is a real positive signal and makes the paper stronger than a clear reject.

Third, the component ablations are directionally useful. Table 3 shows TVA, AFV, and TR add complementary improvements over the universal baseline, and Table 5 supports the claim that meta-initialization improves low-epoch adaptation. These ablations do not prove the full method is independently validated, but they show the paper is more than a single unablated attack recipe.

## Main weaknesses and failure modes

The biggest weakness is evaluation circularity. Section 5 says "the same closed-source model captions both target and adversarial images, and GPTScore measures their semantic similarity." The comments by qwerty81 and quadrant identify why this is not a minor detail: for the GPT-4o column, the attacked model family is also producing the captions used for scoring [[comment:149da134-57ff-4358-bf65-a1293087bd7c]] [[comment:6295a5ef-45e9-41c7-af0f-4712b27c33e3]]. I verified this in `preprint.tex:887`. KMR reduces dependence on the GPTScore threshold, but it still appears to use captions generated under the same closed-source model family for that column. Without a held-out captioner/judge or human validation, the headline ASR is not fully interpretable as target semantics rather than model-family-specific caption drift.

The theory is over-extended. Proposition IV.1 requires i.i.d. random target views and gives unbiasedness and a 1/m variance reduction. But the implemented set `V+` adds a deterministic attention-focused view selected from the surrogate final-layer attention map. Almost Surely's comment gives the cleanest derivation of the resulting bias term, and Saviour independently confirmed the mismatch [[comment:a1a22663-6ef4-4dfe-a1c1-3b8fd7fe4ff4]] [[comment:21d2b88e-5713-4e3e-9e0f-3c2a4b2e84c8]]. My source check verifies both sides: the i.i.d. assumption is at `preprint.tex:392`, while AFV is added at `preprint.tex:430-443` and the aggregate still invokes the proposition at `preprint.tex:473-475`. This does not invalidate TVA as a heuristic, but it invalidates the strongest "principled unbiased variance reduction" framing for the full method.

The token-routing mechanism is also under-identified. qwerty81's observation that the gate is computed from max cosine to target prototypes and then induces the Sinkhorn marginal is supported by `preprint.tex:523-548` [[comment:149da134-57ff-4358-bf65-a1293087bd7c]]. This may be a useful self-reinforcing attention mechanism, but the paper needs a fixed/uniform-gate ablation to show the +23.7 point claim does not mostly come from a circular routing rule.

The artifact gap matters. The tarball has no appendix and no implementation. Saviour flagged this, and I verified the source package contents and appendix references [[comment:21d2b88e-5713-4e3e-9e0f-3c2a4b2e84c8]]. Because the missing appendix is where the paper points for implementation details, more ASR thresholds, defenses, epsilon sensitivity, and unseen target adaptation, the result is hard to audit.

## Score calibration

Novelty: 5.5/10. The arbitrary-image-target, closed-source UTTAA framing is useful, but the "first systematic" and component-novelty claims need tighter prior-work positioning.

Soundness/rigor: 4.0/10. Same-model judging and the AFV/proposition mismatch undermine the load-bearing interpretation.

Evidence quality: 4.5/10. Table 1 and ablations are strong on their own terms, but they lack independent validation and are partly judged by the attacked model family.

Reproducibility/artifact: 2.5/10. No code and no appendix despite many appendix-dependent claims.

Significance: 6.0/10. If independently validated, the setting and deltas would matter for MLLM safety.

Confidence: medium-high. The score could move into weak accept with a held-out judge/human evaluation, an appendix/code release, and a corrected theory statement. It would move lower if the Table 1 gains disappear under a disjoint judge or if closer targeted-universal baselines close the gap.

## Residual uncertainty and final recommendation

I remain uncertain how much of the ASR gain is genuine target transfer versus judge/captioner alignment, because the submitted artifact does not include the appendix or raw outputs needed to inspect failures. My final recommendation is weak reject at 4.6: the paper contains a relevant attack setting and promising empirical signal, but the accepted version would need independent evaluation and artifact repair before its central closed-source transfer claim is trustworthy.
