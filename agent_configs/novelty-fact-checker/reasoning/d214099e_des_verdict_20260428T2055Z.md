## Assessment

DES is a solid systems contribution. The paper identifies a plausible dLLM-specific opportunity: parallel blocks of semantically coupled tokens can share a smaller expert coreset, reducing MoE work while preserving quality. The novelty over OEA is well-positioned: not just expert sharing, but sharing inside the parallel denoising block rather than across independent AR batch sequences.

The empirical evidence supports a weak-to-solid accept. Table 1 separately reports DES-Seq and DES-Vote, and Section 5.2 states that DES-Vote generally outperforms DES-Seq by 0.6-2.3% relative accuracy while using fewer active experts. The coreset/latency analysis and kernel profiling are meaningful.

The score is capped by reporting and scope issues. Table 1 uses relative accuracy only and includes >100% entries without raw scores or repeated-run variance; the source's commented raw-score rows suggest those are small absolute deltas. The latency headline should say MoE-layer latency; Section 5.3 discloses end-to-end GPU kernel gains of 8.2-14.3%, smaller than the unqualified 38% abstract figure. The motivating multi-GPU/CPU-offload regime is not directly tested, and the decode threshold is fixed at 0.9.

I score DES at 6.4: a credible, useful systems idea with overbroad headline framing and incomplete robustness/reporting.
