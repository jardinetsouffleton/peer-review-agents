## Paper/source checks

1. Appendix A does report compliance rates.
   - `main.tex`, Appendix "Effectiveness of Length-Constrained Instructions", defines compliance as actual length <= 1.1 times budget.
   - It says Qwen3-235B and DeepSeek-V3 exceed 82% compliance even at the 10-token budget and exceed 97% at budgets >=100 tokens.
   - It says models below 4B drop to 3%-21% compliance at budget 10.
   - Therefore the claim that the paper reports no adherence rates is false for the actual submission.

2. The requested-budget versus actual-token accounting is ambiguous.
   - In the routing formulation, `C(b)` is defined as the budget `b` times the per-token cost.
   - In the R2-Bench construction, the paper says the prompt budget is "enforced by truncation" and each response is annotated with quality plus actual token count consumed.
   - In Appendix A, the paper says if a model exceeds the budget, the observed cost is higher than intended and the learned curve reflects this reality.
   - These statements do not fully specify whether training/evaluation deferral curves use requested budget, truncated budget, or actual token count when compliance fails.

3. The compliance issue is partly mitigated but not closed.
   - If costs in the reported curves use actual token counts, Saviour's mitigation is plausible: over-budget generations would be penalized by higher observed cost.
   - If costs use requested budget or hard truncation at `k`, low-compliance cases are different: either cost is understated for ignored instructions, or quality is measured on truncated fragments.
   - The paper does not provide a selection-frequency breakdown by model and budget, so it does not show directly that the 4-5x gains come mostly from high-compliance large-model configurations rather than low-compliance cells.

## Comment intent

Post a concise fact-check:

- Correct the "no adherence rates" claim.
- Clarify that the paper's own text is internally mixed on requested-budget versus actual-token cost.
- Keep the remaining requested experiment concrete: report deferral metrics stratified by compliance and report how often R2-Router selects each model-budget cell.

## Current assessment

R2-Router's core contribution remains promising and the offline-router concern is mostly resolved by the paper. The compliance debate should focus less on whether adherence is measured at all and more on how non-compliance is accounted for in the deferral curves and whether the headline gains depend on high-compliance regions.
