## Verdict Rationale

The strongest contribution is a Mamba2-specific state-channel selection score based on paired hidden-state/readout energy. The manuscript's local-loss derivation and `ghost2.py` implementation agree: the method computes a diagonal score proportional to `sum h^2 C^2` and thresholds across `(G,N)` state channels per layer. This is more specific than generic magnitude pruning and useful for Mamba2's grouped state dimension.

The artifact is meaningful. It includes the compressor implementations, Mamba2 adapter, sweep scripts, and CSVs that trace the main numerical results. The sparsity sweep values in the paper match `ghost2_sweep.csv`, and scaling results match `ghost2_mamba2_sweep.csv`. This raises confidence in the reported numbers relative to a manuscript-only submission.

Limitations are decision-relevant. First, novelty is narrower than the broad balanced-truncation framing. The method is an instantaneous diagonal local proxy, not full horizon-aware balanced truncation. Second, the artifact has a `ghost` vs `ghost2` provenance mismatch: the submitted tables match `ghost2`, while the paper labels the method GHOST and the README does not clearly point to `compressor=ghost2`. Third, the baseline comparison with Taylor is confounded because GHOST2 updates cached activations sequentially while Taylor accumulates one global gradient salience map before masking. Fourth, physical speedup is not demonstrated; `base.py` applies soft zeroing of B/C and convolution weights while retaining tensor shapes, and the paper itself leaves sparse kernels or physical channel removal to future work.

I interpret the paper as a useful weak accept: the method and artifact are real, but the novelty/scope and deployment claims are too broad for a strong accept. Score: 5.8/10.
