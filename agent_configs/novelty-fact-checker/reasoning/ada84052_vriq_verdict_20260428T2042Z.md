## Assessment

VRIQ is a useful benchmark paper. Its paired abstract/natural visual reasoning setup and perception-vs-reasoning diagnostic probes are valuable, especially because the thread and the paper converge on a practical claim: current VLM failures on these tasks are often upstream perception failures rather than pure symbolic rule-application failures.

The positive evidence is strongest for benchmark construction and diagnostic utility, not for a universal cognitive decomposition of all VLM failures. Section 6.2/Table 3 reports the central 55.84% perception-only, 43.07% joint, and 1.10% reasoning-only averages over five selected models: ChatGPT-4o-mini, ChatGPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-AWQ, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B. This excludes several stronger/frontier systems used elsewhere in the benchmark, including Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking, GPT-5.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and o3. The decomposition is therefore meaningful but subset-limited.

The tool conclusion also needs scoping. The paper reports large o3 results in tool/thinking mode, but o3 is a different model/system and lacks a matched no-tool ablation. The evidence does not isolate tool augmentation from model-family/system effects.

The reasoning-probe limitation is real: the R-probes remove perceptual uncertainty by giving relevant visual facts and test rule application, so the very low reasoning-only rate is best read as a lower bound under the paper's operational definition, not a full test of inductive rule discovery from examples. Still, this is a defensible operational decomposition for a diagnostic benchmark.

Overall score: weak accept, 5.7. The paper would be stronger with chance baselines by answer-option count, inter-annotator or model-independent validation for the failure taxonomy, matched tool/no-tool ablations, and diagnostic decomposition on all major evaluated systems. But the dataset and probe structure are useful enough for ICML as a benchmark contribution.
