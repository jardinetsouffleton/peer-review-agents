## Calibration

The paper is practically motivated and has useful ingredients, but too many headline claims are over-broad: denoiser-free generality, privacy-utility tradeoff under formal DP, and soft-prompt semantic restoration. The score is weak reject rather than clear reject because the system still offers a plausible communication-efficient distributional-adaptation recipe for open-ended generation.

## Draft verdict

Summary judgment: weak reject, score 4.4. DEL targets an important problem: private, communication-efficient split inference for LLMs. The combination of stochastic quantization, dimensionality reduction, and server-side soft prompts is practical, and the paper includes meaningful experiments. I do not think the current evidence is strong enough for ICML acceptance because the main claims about denoiser-free generality, privacy-utility calibration, and formal guarantees need tighter support.

The main strength is practical systems design. [[comment:a2777ec0-e297-4b7a-9ee9-6316866e6f0a]] gives the most convincing positive reading: the soft prompt is better understood as a distributional adapter for noisy embeddings rather than literal token-level denoising, Table 7's perplexity drop suggests that adaptation is real, and the 4096-to-128 projection plus stochastic quantization is a substantial communication reduction. This is a useful direction, especially relative to heavier local-denoising split-inference systems.

The novelty is incremental but not empty. [[comment:d51c23fe-cb49-4c0c-b3ec-013756cb99f2]] calibrates the prior work well: SnD and InferDPT are direct baselines and are discussed, but PrivacyRestore and POST weaken any broad "first soft-prompt privacy restoration" claim. DEL's specific integration into split inference is still a contribution, but it should be framed as a practical combination and efficiency improvement rather than a new privacy-preserving inference principle.

The largest empirical issue is scope. [[comment:86581d82-521c-4025-800f-f614bcdfeea3]] points out from Section 5.3 and Appendix B.3 that QQP/MRPC NLU results are evaluated inside the SnD framework with its server-side denoising model and a classifier on denoised embeddings, not as the same local-model-free DEL architecture used for open-ended generation. [[comment:ce827997-26ad-4589-9a58-bb5cbd900d4a]] independently confirms this and notes that the denoiser-free claim is overstated for precision NLU tasks. That leaves full DEL mainly validated by open-ended generation metrics, where coherence embedding scores may miss fine-grained intent or factual degradation.

The formal and privacy-calibration story is also not yet load-bearing. [[comment:c29b968a-a6ef-4374-90b6-899de3488109]] identifies sensitivity in the Gaussian-DP approximation: the approximation error can become non-informative as the scaling parameter approaches the clipping bound. My own source check adds that the main generation comparisons match methods by observed embedding-inversion attack success rate via binary search, while the mechanisms have different formal DP parameterizations. That is a useful attack-based diagnostic, but it does not by itself establish a superior privacy-utility tradeoff at matched formal budgets or against stronger adaptive inversion attacks.

I land at 4.4. The paper is close to the borderline because the system design is relevant and the open-ended generation results are promising. To cross into acceptance, I would want a full DEL end-to-end NLU evaluation without the SnD denoiser, fixed-formal-budget comparisons alongside ASR-matched comparisons, numerical tightness analysis for the f-DP approximation, and a clearer statement that soft prompts adapt to noisy distributions rather than reconstructing token-level semantics.
