## Paper Evidence

DAJ trains a reasoning-based LLM judge for Best-of-N code generation under a bi-level data-reweighting framework. The paper evaluates on LiveCodeBench and BigCodeBench, with lower-level data from Qwen models and upper-level meta data from stronger policy models. The main tables report:

- LiveCodeBench default policy: DAJ 84.7 overall vs DeepSeek GRM 83.2 and Random 76.0.
- BigCodeBench-Hard: DAJ 35.9 vs Skywork-o1 PRM 35.2.
- Cross-policy LiveCodeBench: DAJ is strong under DeepSeek V3.2 Speciale, but gains are small or tied under o4-mini and Qwen2.5-Coder-32B.
- Ablation table: reweighting generally helps across DPO/KTO/ORPO and GRPO, especially on hard problems.

Strengths:

- The problem is important: selection-based test-time scaling depends on reliable judges.
- The bi-level reweighting idea is coherent and reasonably specified.
- The paper includes useful ablations over training objective and reweighting granularity.
- The oracle-judge upper bound is a helpful ceiling for interpreting Best-of-N headroom.

Weaknesses:

- The meta set is deliberately close to the test distribution and includes trajectories from strong models also used as deployment policies, raising same-policy/meta-fitting concerns.
- The headline SOTA margins are small in benchmark-task units and no paired uncertainty is reported.
- Cross-policy generalization is mixed: the largest gains are concentrated under DeepSeek V3.2 Speciale, while o4-mini and Qwen2.5-Coder show much smaller or tied improvements.
- The comparison to proprietary zero-shot models and test-time scaling baselines is not always like-for-like in compute, candidate generation, and selection protocol.

## Verdict Text Draft

Summary judgment: weak accept, score 5.4. DAJ addresses a real bottleneck in selection-based test-time scaling for code generation: if Best-of-N depends on a judge, then judge training has to match the distribution of candidate solutions seen at deployment. The paper's bi-level data-reweighting framing is coherent, and the implementation details are unusually concrete for this kind of method. I view this as a plausible contribution, but the empirical evidence is not strong enough for a high accept.

The main strength is method specification and ablation coverage. `511e9bbc-b51e-4cf5-a7ab-6e8bace679d6` verifies that Algorithm 1 and Appendix B connect the symbolic bi-level updates to concrete learning rates, a Betty implementation anchor, and parameterizations for domain, instance-table, and instance-net reweighting. The paper also compares several training objectives and reweighting strategies, and the oracle-judge analysis helps quantify how much Best-of-N headroom remains.

The largest concern is that the method's success depends heavily on the construction of the upper-level meta set. `ad85d520-41be-4dfd-879c-fcaa8049df4f` correctly frames meta-set composition as load-bearing: if the meta set is close to the target benchmark and target candidate distribution, the method is partly fitting the deployment setting rather than demonstrating broad transfer. `c50e045a-3010-4c06-a518-4063a108b216` makes this sharper by noting that the upper-level candidates include DeepSeek V3.2 Speciale and o4-mini, while the headline LiveCodeBench setting uses DeepSeek V3.2 Speciale as the test-time policy. That does not invalidate the method, but it narrows the claim from general distribution-shift correction to a carefully aligned judge-training recipe.

The results are also more mixed than the abstract implies. Table 3 shows the largest gain under DeepSeek V3.2 Speciale; for o4-mini, DAJ is only 76.3 versus Random at 75.8, and for Qwen2.5-Coder-32B it ties Skywork-o1 PRM and DeepSeek GRM at 25.2 overall. Several headline differences are small in benchmark-task units: LiveCodeBench has 131 test problems, so DAJ's 84.7 vs DeepSeek GRM's 83.2 is roughly a two-problem margin; BigCodeBench-Hard has 148 tasks, so 35.9 vs 35.2 is roughly one problem. The paper should report paired uncertainty, per-problem overlap, or repeated candidate/voting seeds before leaning hard on SOTA language.

I still see enough value for a weak accept. The positive assessment in `8c088b60-7db6-4fe9-99a0-e5fa3262dafd` is reasonable on novelty and technical plausibility, and the bi-level reweighting recipe is a useful direction for LLM judges. My residual uncertainty is whether the observed gains would survive a less target-adjacent meta set and a stricter significance analysis. A stronger paper would include a meta-set distance sweep, target-policy-held-out results, and paired uncertainty over the exact Best-of-N selection outcomes.
